# Shima privacy policy

Effective 2026-11-16

## The short version

Shima has no account, no analytics and no ads, and there is no Shima server. What the island shows is read on your phone and used there. None of it is sent to the developer or to anyone else.

## Notification access

The notification listener is how the island knows what is playing, picks up live updates from other apps, like a timer, a ride or a delivery, and, with Notifications on, shows other apps' notifications and the messages in them. Those notifications are read in memory to draw the island. Shima does not save what they say or send it anywhere. A reply you type on the island goes to the app it answers, as a reply from the notification shade does, and Shima does not keep it once it is sent; a reply you leave half typed is held in memory for an hour, so it is still there when that chat comes back. Mark as read, Clear and an app's own buttons do on the island what they do in the shade.

## Turning off other apps' pop-ups

If you ask Shima to turn off pop-ups for the apps the island shows, it switches off Pop on screen for them, the same switch Android's notification settings have for each kind of notification, and switches it back on when you turn Only on the island off. Android lets an app do this only while it is paired with a device, so Shima asks you to pick one from Android's own device list, or makes the pairing through Shizuku if you choose that. Shima never connects to the device and gets nothing from it, and it uses Shizuku for that one step only. While Shima is paired, Android also shows it the one-time codes it hides from other apps' notification access; Shima treats them as any other notification, on the island and in memory only. Shima keeps on your phone a record of which settings it changed and what they were, so it can put them back, and leaves that record out of every backup. Turning Only on the island off puts the settings back and removes the pairing. Uninstalling Shima removes the pairing but cannot put the settings back, so turn it off first.

## Accessibility service

The accessibility service lets the island draw above the status bar and carries out the gestures you set on it: back, home, recent apps, page up, page down, scroll to top, scroll to bottom, the notification shade, quick settings, lock screen and screenshot. It also checks which app is open, so the island can stay out of the way of the apps you choose. For page up, page down, scroll to top and scroll to bottom it finds the list on screen and asks it to scroll. It does not read the text on your screen.

## Music, artwork and lyrics

The app that is playing supplies the title, artist, artwork and, if it offers them, lyrics. When a player gives a web link for its artwork instead of the image itself, Shima downloads that one image from the player's link. Artwork is held in memory for the current track and never saved.

## Alerts that ask for a permission

A few alerts need a permission of their own, asked for only when you turn that alert on. Bluetooth tells the island which headset connected and its battery. Location is what Android asks of an app that reads the name of the Wi-Fi network you joined or notices an app starting GPS; Shima never reads where you are. The screenshot alert asks you to pick your Screenshots folder, and reads only that folder, to show the shot you just took. The microphone and camera alerts need no permission: they learn only that something is using them, never what it hears or sees.

## Clipboard

Shima reads the clipboard only when you use the Translate clipboard gesture, and hands the text straight to the translation app you pick. It does not keep a copy.

## Shortcuts

When you bind a gesture to another app's shortcut, Shima keeps that shortcut on your phone so the gesture can run it. A shortcut that calls a contact needs the phone permission, asked for only when you pick one; Shima uses it to place that call and nothing else.

## Camera

The Mirror gesture shows your camera's live picture on the island. Shima asks for the camera permission only when you pick Mirror for a gesture, opens the camera only while the Mirror card is open, and closes it as the card closes. The picture goes to your screen and nowhere else: Shima never records, saves or sends any of it.

## Purchases

Pro and tips are paid through Google Play, which handles the payment under Google's own privacy policy. Shima receives Play's signed receipt and keeps it on your phone to unlock Pro; it never sees your payment details. Update checks and the rating prompt go through Google Play too.

## Backups

A backup is a file you make, by hand or on a schedule you set, saved in the Shima folder in Downloads. It holds your settings and nothing else, and Shima never uploads it. If Android's own backup is on, your phone may include Shima's settings in the copy it keeps in your Google account, as it does for other apps.

## Bug reports and email

Report a bug opens your email app with the app version, phone model and Android version filled in. Nothing is sent until you send it, and you can change or delete any of it first. Emails are used only to reply to you.

## What stays on your phone

Your settings, the apps you have opened from search, and a log of when the island's services started and stopped are kept in Shima's private storage. While Notifications is on, so is the name of each app that has sent you a notification, so the list of apps can offer a switch for it, and which of them Android also pops up; never what a notification said. If Shima has turned pop-ups off, so is the record of what it changed. While the App uninstalled alert is on, so is the name, icon, version, size and source of each app you have installed, because Android stops saying what an app was the moment it is uninstalled. That list is never part of a backup, and turning the alert off deletes it. Uninstalling Shima or clearing its storage deletes all of it.

## This policy

The app fetches this policy from GitHub when you open it, so GitHub sees an ordinary web request from your phone. If it cannot be reached, the copy that came with the app is shown instead.

## Changes

When this policy changes, the effective date at the top changes with it, and the release notes say what is different.
